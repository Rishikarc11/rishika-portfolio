01The problem

Planning a celebration starts with a picture in your head and no way to show it to anyone. Mood boards take hours, planners cost thousands, and vendors quote against a brief you struggled to write. Most people with a garden, a living room or a rooftop never get past "I'd love to do something here."

EventCulture's brief was an AI-first marketplace for hosts in the UK: turn a photo of the actual space and a sentence about the feeling into five photorealistic concepts, then connect the host to decorators, florists and planners who can make the chosen one real. I designed the host website, the generation flow, and the vendor dashboard that receives the leads.

Two sides, one promise

Hosts get a picture before they commit to anything. Vendors get a lead that arrives with the space, the brief, the budget and the concept already attached, instead of a vague enquiry. The product only works if both halves hold.

03A photo and a feeling, not a form

The usual way to brief an event tool is a form: occasion, guest count, style dropdown, colour picker. It produces briefs nobody would say out loud. EventCulture asks for two things instead. A photo of the real space, with three tips on how to take it. And a sentence about how it should feel, written the way you'd describe it to a friend: "Like a Sunday lunch that never wants to end."

The structured details, occasion, guests, budget, date and location, sit in a quiet side panel so they never become the main event. Then the system shows what it understood from the sentence, as tags: intimate scale, Mediterranean palette, candlelight, slow music. If it read you wrong, you fix it before anything is generated.

Rejected: a style quiz with image pairs. Faster to build, but it would have given every host the same six aesthetics and thrown away the one thing that makes the result theirs: their own room.

04Make the ninety seconds visible

Ninety seconds is a long time to stare at a spinner, and long enough for people to doubt anything is happening. So the generation step is a live log: reading your space dimensions and lighting, parsing your feeling brief, matching colour palette and material world, drafting five layouts, matching vendors near you. Each line completes with what it found. The wait becomes evidence that the system looked at your photo, not a stock template.

The results page keeps the same honesty. Five concepts, each with a match percentage and the line from your brief it answers, shown next to your space as it is. None quite right? Tweak the brief or regenerate, free, no limit.

05The concept is a bridge to a vendor, not the product

A pretty render is a dead end unless someone can build it. So every concept carries two actions: save to moodboard, and see vendors. Step five shows decorators and stylists already matched to the concept and pre-quoted, with Send Brief as the primary action. The brief they receive is the host's photo, sentence, details and chosen concept, so the first conversation starts from a shared picture.

Consent runs the other way. Vendors see a host's real details only once the host decides to reach out, and the pricing page says so in plain words: free to try, bookings cost only what the vendor quotes. On the vendor side, the dashboard is built around that lead: inquiries, messages, bookings and a calendar, with verification and portfolio so hosts can trust who they're talking to.

06Craft and system

  • Cultural celebrations are a first-class category. Diwali, Lunar New Year, Eid, Quinceañera, Bar and Bat Mitzvah and Onam sit beside weddings and birthdays on the home page, because for a UK audience they are not a niche.
  • Pricing that matches the promise. Free gives three concepts a month. Premium at £9.99 removes the limit and adds moodboard sharing. Elite at £29.99 adds a human planner. Enterprise is a waitlist, not a fake tier.
  • One system, two audiences. Host and vendor surfaces share components and type, so a vendor viewing a host's moodboard sees the same cards the host built it with.

07Outcome

The host website, generation flow and vendor dashboard reached dev-ready, with responsive and mobile views for every host flow and a shared design system. The project was dropped before launch when the company wound down.

What I'd have measured

  • Upload to first concept. How many hosts who start the flow see five concepts. The whole front half exists to move this.
  • Concept to Send Brief. Whether a picture actually turns into a vendor conversation.
  • Vendor response time on pre-briefed leads. The proof that an attached concept makes a lead worth answering.
